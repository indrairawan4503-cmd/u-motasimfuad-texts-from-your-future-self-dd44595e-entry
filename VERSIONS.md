# Version log — variant B (password concept)

Concept: future self texts asking for the password you are about to
change — and already knows it. One concrete scene (locked out of email)
+ one twist (he already knows the new password because he watched you
type it). Iterated on the full byte budget; gate is 1–200 characters.

- v1 (142c): "Hey, it's you from 2031. Before you change your password,
  text me the new one — I'm locked out and I need it." Plain ask, no
  proof it is really you, no punchline. Too generic.
- v2 (161c): added the scene — "locked out of our email again".
  Still no twist; reads as a normal password-reset text.
- v3 (172c): added "Relax, I already know it." The twist arrives,
  but with nothing proving it — the claim hangs unsupported.
- v4 (178c): added "I watched you type it." Proof of the twist,
  but flat as a single clause; no comic beat at the end.
- v5 (183c): staccato punchline — "Twice. Very slowly." Lands on a
  single read, but still no detail that could only come from this
  person's life (judge criterion 3 is weak).
- v6 (212c): tried naming the detail another way — "Biscuit plus the
  year you met Sara" — over the 200-char gate. Cut.
- v7 (207c): tried "Biscuit's vet needs the records" as the scene —
  also over budget, and the vet detour slows the punchline. Cut.
- v8 (196c, FINAL): names the old password itself as proof —
  "Biscuit2019" (dog's name + year, a detail only this person knows).
  Ask + proof + twist all inside budget, punchline last:
  "I watched you type it. Twice. Very slowly."

Final (196 chars):
"Hey, it's you from 2031. Before you change your password, text me the new one — I'm locked out of our email again. Relax, I already know it: Biscuit2019. I watched you type it. Twice. Very slowly."
